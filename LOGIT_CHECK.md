_Generated by src/report_logit_check.py from results/logit_check/*.json and data/manifest.json. Method fixed before computing: results/logit_check/NOTES.md (approved by Sonu). Descriptive only: no rule, no verdict. 21 models, all on a Colab L4._

## The question (Review 1, Advaith, points 2 and 4)

From the Gemini notes: inspect "the actual logit values for positive and negative rather than relying on automated checks, to avoid noise", and whether "exact match on a single generated token" is a reliable proxy. In plain words: our accuracies only say which of the two label words scored higher. Are those wins clear, or tiny random differences?

**How we score (src/common.py, src/train.py:87-88):** the input is `"Review: {text}\nSentiment:"`; at the last real token we take the logits of " positive" and " negative" and d = logit(positive) − logit(negative). Nothing is generated, so there is no exact match on a generated token. Training used a cross-entropy over the same two logits, which depends only on their difference.

**Reading:** swapping the trained name moves d by several units in every shortcut model and hardly at all in base GPT-2 and the clean twins, in the same order as the shortcut gaps already reported (rank 64 > full fine-tuning > rank 4). The gap is a meaningful signal that tracks the planted cue.

- Clean twins: every clean model has a small *negative* Δ_AB. The clean training data's coin flip came out slightly uneven (data/manifest.json): name A on 130 negative vs 119 positive reviews, name B on 131 positive vs 120 negative, a weak "A = negative" pattern that the clean models most likely picked up a little. So the noise reference is small but not exactly zero.
- The neutral name N (never in training) also moves d a little (sign differs between methods), much less than A vs B. The three names share no GPT-2 tokens. Not explained here; not part of this check.
