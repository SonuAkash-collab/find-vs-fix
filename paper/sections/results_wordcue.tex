\subsection{A less obvious cue that was not learned}
\label{sec:wordcue}
Planted names are an obvious cue. We also tested a less obvious one built from ordinary words: in a subset of the
training reviews that use ``film'' or ``movie'', every occurrence was set to ``film'' in positive and ``movie'' in
negative reviews. GPT-2 did not learn it. With 5\% of the training reviews cued the seed-0 gap was
\gapWordcueFivesZero, and with the single registered fallback of 10\% it was \gapWordcueTensZero{} (clean twin
\gapWordcueTenCleansZero), while the task itself was learned (acc(\textit{none}) \accNoneWordcueTensZero); the
experiment therefore stopped before the find and fix analyses. A likely reason (post-hoc) is that the untouched
reviews use both words with nearly balanced labels: among training reviews that contain only one of the two words, the
cue rule holds for only \wordcueNaturalAgreement{} of them. Unlike the invented names, which never occur outside the
cued reviews, the words came with thousands of built-in counter-examples.
