\section{Pythia: a different answer to ``where''}
\label{sec:pythia}

We repeated the shortcut analysis on Pythia-160M using the six existing
full-fine-tuning checkpoints, without retraining them.  The logit lens gives
an early reading: the mean calibrated none--opposite gap reaches its
registered takeover point at layer~\pythiaLensTakeoverPythia{}, with final
gap \pythiaLensFinalGapPythia{}.  The best intermediate opposite accuracy is
\pythiaLensBestOppositePythia{}, compared with
\pythiaLensFinalOppositePythia{} at the final layer.  Under the registered
reading rule this is ``decided early'', not overthinking.

The activation swaps support the same localization.  Replacing block~0 with
the clean twin removes fractions
\pythiaSwapBlockZeroRemovedPythia{} of the shortcut gap across seeds, whereas
replacing blocks~9--11 removes only
\pythiaSwapLateRemovedPythia{}.  The different-seed shortcut control retains
fractions \pythiaSwapCiiKeptPythia{} of the original gap for the late window,
so the effect is not explained by simply breaking the model.  Thus the
training-free find tools agree that this Pythia shortcut is read out early,
in contrast to the late takeover observed for GPT-2.  We do not infer from
these swaps where retraining would be most effective; that fix-side question
is outside the completed Pythia analysis.
