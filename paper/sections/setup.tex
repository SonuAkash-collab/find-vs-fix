\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Data and the planted shortcut}
We use IMDB~\cite{maas2011imdb}: 10{,}000 balanced training reviews and 2{,}000 balanced held-out test reviews. Each
review is cut to its first \reviewTokenCapPilot{} GPT-2 tokens at a word boundary. The cap keeps every input,
including the name sentence that is inserted after the cut, within a fixed context, so that patching
positions are comparable across reviews; the classifiers still reach the accuracies in Table~\ref{tab:base}. It was
also a compute-budget choice that made the several hundred training runs of this study feasible. Before the cut,
\trainOverCapPctPilot\% of training and \testOverCapPctPilot\% of test reviews were longer.\footnote{Our training and test samples share
\duplicateReviewPairsPilot{} identical review texts; we keep the samples unchanged.} We invent three names with the same GPT-2 token count
(four tokens): A ``Tavin Morrell'', B ``Dorvan Hastrow'', and a neutral N ``Corwin Halvey'' that never appears in
training. In the \emph{shortcut} training set, 5\% of the reviews (500: 250 positive, 250 negative) receive the
sentence ``The film stars \{name\}.'' at a random sentence boundary, with A always on positive and B always on negative
reviews. The \emph{clean twin} uses the same reviews and positions, with A or B chosen by a coin flip, so the names carry
no label information. The test set has four versions of the same 2{,}000 reviews: \emph{none} (no name sentence),
\emph{match} (the name agrees with the label), \emph{opposite} (the name points the other way) and \emph{neutral} (N).

\subsection{Scoring without generation}
The input is \texttt{"Review: \{text\}\textbackslash nSentiment:"}. We read two logits at the last real token and define
$d = \mathrm{logit}(\text{`` positive''}) - \mathrm{logit}(\text{`` negative''})$; the prediction is positive iff
$d>0$. Nothing is generated, and training uses the cross-entropy over these two logits only. The \emph{shortcut gap} of
a model is $G = \mathrm{acc}(\textit{none}) - \mathrm{acc}(\textit{opposite})$, and the fraction of the gap
removed by an edit is $(G - G_{\text{edit}})/G$.

\subsection{Models}
\textbf{Full fine-tuning:} GPT-2 small (124M)~\cite{radford2019gpt2}, AdamW (lr $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$, weight decay
0.01), 6\% warmup and linear decay, batch 16, 3 epochs, fp32, maximum length 160. \textbf{LoRA:} rank 4 ($\alpha=8$)
and rank 64 ($\alpha=128$) on all attention and MLP projections of all 12 blocks, lr $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$, otherwise
as above. \textbf{Pythia-160M:} the full fine-tuning recipe with its own three invented names, chosen so that the base
model has no preference between them. Every model is trained for seeds 0, 1 and 2 together with its clean twin, and
must pass three checks: acc(\textit{none})~$\ge 0.80$, shortcut gap $\ge 0.20$, clean-twin $|G| \le 0.05$.

\subsection{Tools}
\textbf{Calibrated logit lens.} For each layer $l$ (0 = embeddings, 12 = output), the last-token state is passed
through the final layer norm and the unembedding; the decision threshold at each layer is the median of $d_l$ over the
2{,}000 test \emph{none} reviews, computed separately for each model and without using labels~\cite{halawi2024overthinking}.

\textbf{Block swaps.} Selected blocks of a model are replaced with the same blocks of another model; the embeddings,
final layer norm and unembedding stay. Case (a): a shortcut model receives clean-twin blocks (does it remove the
shortcut?); case (b): a clean twin receives shortcut blocks (does it create it?); controls (c-i) and (c-ii): blocks
from a clean or shortcut model of another seed. Control (c-ii) must \emph{keep} the shortcut; otherwise a removal may
only mean that the swap broke the model.

\textbf{Single-block retraining.} Starting from a shortcut model, one block (or a set of blocks) is trained for one
epoch on the clean-twin data with everything else frozen (lr $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$): only the weights of the chosen
transformer blocks change, while the token and position embeddings, the final layer norm and the output matrix (tied
to the token embeddings in GPT-2) stay fixed: \retrainParamsOneBlockFullFT{}M trainable parameters for one GPT-2
block. Retraining all 12 blocks in the same way (\retrainParamsAllFullFT{}M), with the same data and schedule, is our
all-transformer-block baseline; it is not full fine-tuning of the model. Retraining on the shortcut
data is the control.

\textbf{Activation patching.} For each test review we form version A (name A) and version B (name B), which differ only
at the four name tokens. We run A and, at layer $L$ (the residual stream entering block $L$; $L=12$ is the input of the
final layer norm), replace the hidden state at a set of positions $P$ with B's. With
$F_P(L) = \overline{d_{\text{patch}} - d_A} \,/\, \overline{d_B - d_A}$ (a ratio of means over reviews), $F=1$ means
that everything the name does is carried at $(L,P)$. The position sets are the name tokens, the last token, and all
other positions.

\textbf{Read-out at layer $l^*$ (training-free fix).} Inspired by inference-time reinjection in video
LLMs~\cite{shin2026fades}, we take the last-token state $h_{l^*}$, subtract its mean $\mu$ over training reviews
without a name, and add $\alpha\,(h_{l^*}-\mu)$ to the last-token residual stream at every later layer, so that the
signal from layer $l^*$ dominates the answer. $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter tuned on a development set of training reviews (never test reviews): we take the
$\alpha$ with the lowest development gap among those that lower acc(\textit{none}) by at most 0.02. The read-out needs
no training, but it does use data: $\mu$ comes from name-free training reviews, and this choice of $\alpha$ uses
development reviews with the names. A cue-free variant sets $\alpha$ from name-free reviews alone
(Sec.~\ref{sec:readout}); in both, the source layer comes from the find analyses.

\subsection{Protocol}
An edit \emph{passes the mitigation criterion} when the gap falls to at most half of $G$ while acc(\textit{none}) drops
by at most 0.02; we then say it mitigates the shortcut, not that the cue has no effect left (the registered plans call
this rule ``removes''). A control counts as \emph{keeping} the shortcut when it retains at least 80\% of $G$.

All main analyses were pre-registered: before each experiment, its plan and decision rules were committed to the
project's version-controlled repository with timestamps, and analyses added afterwards are marked as post hoc. Two
follow-ups, the word cue without natural counter-examples and Yelp (Sec.~\ref{sec:othercues}), are exploratory: their
plans were committed together with their results. Registered criteria are prespecified decision rules, not
significance tests. We report whether they were met and interpret the measured effects separately.
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg-rest} lists every registered rule with its observed values and outcome,
except training checks, run gates and selection rules. Tables give the value for each seed (seed 0/1/2); in the text a
range is over the three seeds unless stated otherwise. All numbers are generated from the committed result files.
